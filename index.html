[Ongoing Research]

Meteorological-to-Groundwater Drought Propagation in Bangladesh: Regional Contrasts, Temporal Change and CMIP6 Projections

Key points: Studies rainfall-to-groundwater drought in three contrasting zones: Barind (dry, clay, heavily pumped), Sylhet (wet basin), and CHT (hilly). Simple matching gives 0–5 months lag but from only 5–11 events. Model-inferred effective lag (265 points per zone) finds sharp shifts: Barind April 2011, CHT April 2013, Sylhet September 2014. Barind shows longer memory, consistent with slow recharge. Future projections are drier after correction, with about one-third of late-century conditions outside past experience.


[Manuscript in Preparation]

Seasonal ENSO Signals, Annual Flood Peaks, and Changing Stage–Discharge Relations in the Ganges and Brahmaputra Rivers of Bangladesh

Mohammad Najmus Sakib, Rounak Afroz

Key points: Studies the Ganges at Hardinge Bridge and the Brahmaputra at Bahadurabad. The known seasonal link between ENSO and Ganges flow still holds when extended to 2022. However, the same ENSO signal does not predict the largest flood of the year. Gauges themselves changed over time: Hardinge Bridge shifts around 2006 with falling water level at same flow, Bahadurabad rises then falls around 2012, while rainfall shows no such change. A strong seasonal link does not mean a strong yearly-peak link.

[Undergrad Thesis - Ongoing]

Multi-Century Monthly Streamflow Reconstruction and Drought-Flood Abrupt Alternation Event Analysis of the Brahmaputra River

Under supervision of Dr. Rounak Afroz

The Brahmaputra is one of the largest rivers in South Asia, and its floods regularly affect millions of people in the delta region of Bangladesh. The instrumental record at Bahadurabad is too short to capture long-term change in the river. This project reconstructs centuries of monthly discharge at Bahadurabad using tree rings. Tree-ring width reflects the moisture a tree received each year, among other factors. Using this, tree-ring width is matched against the known flow of the river. This builds a weighted statistical model. The model then estimates past discharge, extending the record beyond what is currently available. This reconstruction will then be used to analyze historical droughts, wet periods, and abrupt drought-flood transitions. The result gives planners and researchers a longer baseline for assessing hydrological extremes and flood risk in downstream Bangladesh.

FLOOD SUSCEPTIBILITY ASSESSMENT IN THE SATKHIRA DISTRICT OF BANGLADESH THROUGH MULTI-CRITERION ANALYSIS WITH AHP AND REMOTE SENSING

This study maps flood risk in Satkhira District using GIS with the Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP). Ten factors were used: elevation, slope, drainage density, distance from rivers, distance from roads, rainfall, land use/land cover, soil type, NDVI, and TWI.

Key points: Most of the district is at moderate (57.1%) or high (35.1%) risk, with 0.5% extreme, 7.3% low, and less than 0.1% very low. In total, 92.7% falls in the moderate-to-extreme range. Validation against the 2015 flood gives AUC 0.806, better than the literature-based weighting (AUC 0.782). The model finds almost all flooded areas (recall 99.47%) but is deliberately cautious, with lower precision (32.40%) and accuracy (36.98%), which suits precautionary planning. Results point to needs for embankment management, controlled land use, and flood-resilient infrastructure.

Mohammad Najmus Sakib, Maesha Mashiyat
